## The Counterintuitive Move

The world's most celebrated long-term investor spent six months selling half of his favorite stock—while that stock was trading near all-time highs.

In 2024, Warren Buffett reduced Berkshire Hathaway's Apple position from **789 million shares** to **300 million shares**—a reduction of **61.9%** across two consecutive quarters. Apple's fundamentals hadn't deteriorated. iPhone demand remained resilient. Services revenue was compounding. There was no earnings collapse, no scandal, no broken thesis.

So what was this actually about?

The answer is more useful—and more applicable to your own portfolio—than most people realize.

## The Real Driver: Concentration Risk, Not Conviction Risk

The instinctive read—Buffett turned bearish on Apple—doesn't hold up against the data.

**He kept 300 million shares.** If Buffett had genuinely soured on Apple, a full exit would be far more consistent with his style. Keeping 21.67% of a multi-hundred-billion-dollar portfolio in one stock is not the behavior of someone who has lost faith.

**He simultaneously initiated a position in Alphabet (GOOGL).** Buying a different tech company while selling one is a rotation, not a retreat. The thesis didn't flip to "avoid tech"—it became "which tech is worth owning at this valuation."

**The real mechanism was concentration discipline.**

When a single position approaches 50% of a multi-hundred-billion-dollar portfolio managed for millions of beneficiaries, the question stops being "do I believe in this company?" and starts being "what happens to everything else if this falls 30%?"

At 48% weight, a 30% Apple drawdown would have erased roughly 14-15 percentage points from total portfolio value in a single move. That is an unacceptable tail risk for a fiduciary, regardless of the underlying business quality.

> **The reusable framework**: Even your highest-conviction position needs a ceiling. Not because you think it will fall, but because you can't afford the consequences if it does.

**The common thread**: Buffett isn't chasing high-multiple growth. He's concentrating in businesses that sit inside unavoidable daily consumer behaviors—a credit card you swipe every day, a beverage you drink every day, a rating agency embedded in every bond deal. The Apple reduction didn't break this logic. It returned the portfolio to it.

## Risk Factors and Boundaries

Before acting on any of this, the following caveats are material:

**⚠️ 13F data carries an approximately 45-day lag.** This analysis is based on Q4 2025 13F filings, reflecting holdings as of December 31, 2025. Current positions (as of March 2026) may have changed materially.

**The tax explanation cannot be dismissed.** Buffett publicly cited expectations around U.S. capital gains tax policy as a contributing factor in the Apple reduction. If tax optimization was the primary driver, the concentration-discipline framing above is supplementary, not conclusive.

**Past behavior does not predict future decisions.** The operations analyzed here reflect judgments made through 2024. Apple's AI strategy, Services margin expansion, and other 2025 developments are variables that were not yet fully visible during the sell-down.

**This is not investment advice.** All analysis is based on publicly available 13F filings, provided for research purposes only. Nothing here constitutes a buy or sell recommendation.

## Actionable Takeaways

**1. Set a single-position ceiling (position sizing discipline)**
> Regardless of conviction level, consider capping any single holding at 20-25% of your portfolio. Buffett's Apple reduction can be read as a ceiling being enforced—not a thesis being abandoned.

**2. Separate "judgment" from "structure" (decision framework)**
> When you sell something, ask: did my view of this business change, or does my portfolio structure require adjustment? These have different implications.

**3. Track behavior nodes, not prices (monitoring method)**
> The next data point: the Q1 2026 13F (expected ~May 2026) will reveal whether Apple was further reduced, held, or rebuilt. AXP's trajectory matters more than its daily price.
